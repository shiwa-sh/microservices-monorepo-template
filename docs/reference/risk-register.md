# Risk Register

Every ADR discloses the risks its own decision accepts, and each disclosure is complete for its subject. What no ADR can see is the total: a reader learns the cookie scope in one document, the header trust in another, and the detection latency in a third, and nothing composes them.

This document is that composition. It adds no risk and re-decides none — every row links to the decision that accepted it, which remains the only place the reasoning lives.

**Severity is impact against the compensating control**, not likelihood. A risk with a strong control and a catastrophic reach outranks one with a weak control and a bounded reach, because the first is the one that ends badly when the control fails.

| 1 | **A product-origin XSS rides an operator session into the ops tier.** One session cookie is scoped to the parent host, so it reaches every `*.ops.<host>` origin | every operator tool: the cluster's control surface, its dashboards, and its data inspector | the product-origin CSP — a header, not a boundary — plus per-tool authorization and AAL2 on the ops tier | a non-first-party origin under the apex, or the product surface rendering user-supplied content to other users | [ADR-0306](../adr/0306-trust-tiers-and-urls.md) |

## What the composition shows

**Rows 3, 6, and 7 are one risk.** Nothing pages, the regression window is a day, and the alerting stack's own failure is invisible — together they mean the platform's mean time to detect is set by human attention, and its floor is the next working day. [`detection-latency.md`](detection-latency.md) is that composition stated as a table, and it is the artefact an availability objective is checked against.

**Rows 1 and 2 are the security pair.** Both trade a boundary for simplicity, both are compensated by controls that are strong until they are not, and both have seams with triggers this platform can observe. They are the two rows to read before deciding this template fits a project.

**Rows 8 and 13 are the build path's two unbounded residuals**, and [`build-path.md`](build-path.md) is that composition stated as a table — the merge-path counterpart to `detection-latency.md`. Reading the two together gives the pair of dominant terms: run-time exposure is set by human attention, and merge-time exposure by whether a property is machine-visible.

**Row 5 is the only self-inflicted one**, and it is deliberate: a gate that cannot be bypassed is a gate that can lock the platform out. It stays because the alternative — enforcing image provenance where the deploying party can route around it — is not enforcement ([ADR-0203](../adr/0203-policy-enforcement.md)).

**A risk leaves this register in one of two ways.** Its trigger fires and the decision is retaken, or the decision is amended and the risk stops being accepted. Nothing leaves by being fixed quietly, because the row is a property of a decision rather than of the code.
